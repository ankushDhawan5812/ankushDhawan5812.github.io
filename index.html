Ankush Dhawan

Hello! My name is Ankush, and I am a second-year PhD student in Robotics at Stanford University. In my undergrad at Stanford, I worked with Prof. Monroe Kennedy III and Prof. Karen Liu on learning for manipulation and novel robotic hardware. Previously, I worked with Dr. Nathaniel Hanson at the MIT Lincoln Laboratory Human Resilience and Technology Group on soft robotics for disaster response. I also have experience in industry, across companies small and large, designing and testing a wide range of engineering products (Tesla, Opal Camera, Embark Trucks). Outside of research, I love teaching: I helped create Stanford's CS 123 in 2023, have taught it six times, and received a 2026 Centennial Teaching Assistant Award for that work.

Publications

I'm interested in robotics, tactile sensing, and dexterous manipulation. My work spans from hardware design to learning methodologies for robotic systems.

Real2Sem2Real: 3D Semantic Augmentations for Robot Learning from a Single Human RGB Demonstration
Matthew Strong*, Ankush K. Dhawan*, Julien Poffet, Giuse Pham, Jinho So, Monroe Kennedy III
arXiv preprint, 2026
Website arXiv Post

Turns one monocular RGB video of a human demonstration into thousands of simulated robot demonstrations across objects of the same semantic category, using DINOv3-distilled Gaussian splat tracking and digital-cousin alignment. Policies trained on a single human video per task match or exceed teleoperation-trained policies on two robots and four tasks.

VisTacAlign: Co-Training Dexterous Policies on Tactile Human and Robot Demonstrations using Visual-Tactile Alignment
Julien Poffet, Matthew Strong, Ankush K. Dhawan, Baiyu Shi, Shalika Neelaveni, Yujia Yuan, Zhenan Bao, Monroe Kennedy III
Under review for ICRA, 2027
Website arXiv Post

Aligns human demonstrations from a capacitive tactile glove with robot data across vision, touch, and kinematics to co-train a visuotactile diffusion policy. Adding aligned human demos lifts Lego assembly success from 10% to 80% and enables strawberry plucking across all fruit sizes.

Gotta Grow Fast: Design and Benchmarking of a Tip Mount for High-Speed Vine Robots
Antonio Alvarez Valdivia, Robert Reeve, Ankush K. Dhawan, Ciera McFarland, Chad Council, Margaret McGuinness, Nathaniel Hanson
IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L), 2026
Website Paper arXiv

A triangular roller tip mount that carries sensors and tools on soft vine robots growing through tip eversion, with a benchmarking testbed that characterizes how mounting approaches affect growth speed and internal resistance.

Dynamic Layer Detection of a Thin Silk Cloth using DenseTact Optical Tactile Sensors
Ankush K. Dhawan*, Camille Chungyoun*, Karina Ting*, Monroe Kennedy III
IROS, 2025
Website Video Paper

Transformer-based cloth layer detection using DenseTact 2.0 optical tactile sensors. Achieves 98.21% accuracy in classifying grasped cloth layers through anthropomorphic rubbing motion.

Field Insights for Portable Vine Robots in Urban Search and Rescue
Ankush K. Dhawan*, Ciera McFarland*, Rhea Kumari, Chad Council, Margaret Coad, Nathaniel Hanson
SSRR, 2024
Video Paper

Experiments with the SPRoUT vine robot in engineered collapse structures for urban search and rescue scenarios. Tests viability, control, and sensorization of soft growing robots.

DenseTact-Mini: An Optical Tactile Sensor for Grasping Multi-Scale Objects From Flat Surfaces
Won Kyung Do, Ankush K. Dhawan, Mathilda Kitzmann, Monroe Kennedy III
ICRA, 2024 Best Paper Finalist
Website Video Paper

Soft optical tactile fingertip enabling tap, rolling, and fingernail grasping strategies. Demonstrates >90% success rate across small and thin objects including 1 mm basil seeds and thin paperclips.

Core-double shell composite for lead removal from aqueous solutions
Ankush K. Dhawan, Jeffery W. Seyler, Brian C. Bohrer
Heliyon, 2019
Paper

A core-double shell composite consisting of a sand base substrate with sequential layers of chitosan and graphene oxide for Pb(II) adsorption from aqueous solutions. The material achieved lead concentrations below the EPA limit (<15 ppb), with adsorption characteristics following the Freundlich isotherm model.

Teaching

CS 123: A Hands-On Introduction to Building AI-Enabled Robots

Head Teaching Assistant, Stanford University Centennial TA Award, 2026

With Hands-On Robotics and Prof. Karen Liu, I helped create CS 123: A Hands-On Introduction to Building AI-Enabled Robots in 2023, transforming a Robotics Club project into an undergraduate introductory AI robotics course built around Pupper. I have now taught it six times as Head Teaching Assistant, and teaching it is one of my favorite parts of being at Stanford. For this work, I received Stanford's Centennial Teaching Assistant Award in 2026. We have also been featured by the Stanford Report.

The course teaches robotics fundamentals through hands-on construction and programming, covering motor control, kinematics, and walking algorithms. Students also explore cutting-edge topics in reinforcement learning, computer vision, and large language models for robotics. The course has now taught over 200 students, and each quarter culminates in a demo day where students showcase their projects to industry professionals and robotics researchers from academia.

ENGR 108: Introduction to Matrix Methods

Teaching Assistant, Stanford University · Students from freshman through graduate level

ENGR 108 covers the fundamentals of vectors and matrices, solving linear equations, least-squares methods, and their applications. The course emphasizes practical implementation using the Julia programming language, with applications spanning tomography, image processing, data fitting, time series prediction, and finance.
